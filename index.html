IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

Reading a Line Graph by Moonlight

Structure, overviews, two model essays and the language to write them.

Press → or swipe the arrows below. The moons change by themselves.

Speaking warm-up: Part 1

Answer out loud in 20–35 seconds first. Then click to compare with a sample. Do not memorise it word for word.

Speaking warm-up: Part 3

Use four steps: position, explanation, qualification, implication. Pick a topic, answer aloud, then reveal the sample.

What IELTS looks for

Do not just read out numbers. Choose the main features, spot trends and compare.

Task Achievement

Summarise main features, give an overview, use relevant figures.

Coherence and Cohesion

Organise logically and link ideas well.

Lexical Resource

Wide, accurate vocabulary without repetition.

Grammatical Range

Varied structures, consistently accurate.

Look for these in every line graph

Trends, extremes (highest and lowest), fluctuations, sharp changes, turning points, and intersections where one line overtakes another.

The four-paragraph structure

Tap a paragraph.

No conclusion needed. Your overview already summarises the main features.

Introduction and overview

Introduction: paraphrase, one sentence

The [graph type] illustrates / compares + what is measured + categories + time period.

Shows → illustrates, depicts. Percentage → proportion, rate. From 2004 to 2008 → between 2004 and 2008.

Overview: two sentences, no detail

Sentence 1: the most significant overall trends. Sentence 2: the key contrast, extreme, turning point or change in position.

Five-question test

Which line changes most? Who is highest or lowest at the start and end? What fluctuates or stays stable? Any peaks, troughs or crossings? Which two features make this graph different from any other?

Never invent reasons. Say what happened, not why.

Seven patterns to spot

Model essay 1: unemployment, 2004–2008

Grammar to notice

Result participle: "rose dramatically, making it the highest". Contrast: whereas / while. before + -ing for two changes in a row. despite + -ing. the latter for the second of two items.

Model essay 2: overseas visitors, 1987–2007

Grammar to notice

Having risen (perfect participle), with the figure falling (with + noun + -ing), this was followed by (passive), had recovered (past perfect only when it adds meaning).

Vocabulary bank

Match the word to the size of the change.

Up

rise, grow, climb, surge, soar, rocket, edge up, recover

Down

fall, decline, drop, dip, plummet, plunge, slide, ease

Steady or shaky

remain stable, level off, plateau, fluctuate, oscillate

Peaks and lows

peak at, reach a high of, hit a low of, bottom out, rebound

Numbers

from X to Y, by X, at X, just over, just under, nearly, twice as high as, respectively

Linkers

Overall, By contrast, Whereas, Meanwhile, Subsequently, By the end of the period

Percentage or percentage points?

2% to 4% is a rise of 2 percentage points, or 100% relative to the start. Usually the first is enough.

Write it in five steps

1. Spend 2–3 minutes reading the graph: units, dates, start, end, peaks, crossings.

2. Write a one-sentence introduction.

3. Write a two-sentence overview.

4. Write two body paragraphs, grouping the data by pattern and using selected figures.

5. Spend 2 minutes checking figures, tenses, articles and word count (150 minimum).

Common mistakes

Listing numbers with no analysis. No overview. Inventing reasons. Using "5%" when you mean "5 percentage points". Wrong tense. A conclusion that repeats the overview. Overloading sentences.

A Band 9-style essay is accurate, clear and well grouped, not full of fancy words.
